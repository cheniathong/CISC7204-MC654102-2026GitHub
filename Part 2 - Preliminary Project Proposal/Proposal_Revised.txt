1. Project topic and research question

Region: England. Domain: sports and football analytics. The project
examines the 2025/26 English Premier League using match records and
player statistics. The principal question is:

    How are team attacking and defensive indicators associated with
    league points during the 2025/26 season?

Two supporting questions are examined. First, are the differences in
attacking activity between home and away matches consistent across
teams? Second, after considering position and playing time, does
player age relate to scoring output? A preliminary look at the
season-2526 data already suggests that attacking and defensive
indicators correlate with points at similar magnitudes (Pearson
r ≈ +0.91 for goals scored per match, r ≈ −0.90 for goals conceded
per match); the report therefore compares the strength and direction
of these associations rather than presuming either side is more
important.


2. Project purpose

Outcomes. The project connects the course workflow — importing,
cleaning, exploring and visualising data — to a concrete football
question. It will produce a reproducible notebook and an evidence-
based account of what the available data can and cannot show.

Skills. The work practises Pandas filtering, data-type conversion,
duplicate and missing-value checks, team-name reconciliation, group
aggregation and joins. On the analysis side it develops chart
selection, correlation analysis, paired statistical comparisons and
the interpretation of uncertainty.

Knowledge. The project builds understanding of three distinctions
that are easy to conflate in football data: player-, match- and
team-level observations; playing-time exposure and its effect on
scoring rates; and association versus causation. Comparing two
sources also illustrates why apparently similar football indicators
may use different definitions.


3. Datasets and scope

Dataset 1 — season-2526.csv.
The file contains 380 match records for the 20 Premier League clubs,
dated 15 August 2025 to 24 May 2026. Fields include team names,
full-time and half-time scores and results, referees, shots, shots
on target, corners, fouls and cards. Each team has 38 match
appearances. Points are calculated from results using three for a
win and one for a draw; administrative deductions are not applied.
Format matches the football-data.co.uk historical-results
convention (columns FTHG, FTAG, HS, AS, HST, AST, HC, AC, HY, AY,
HR, AR).

4. Project tasks and deliverables

4.1 Data inspection and cleaning
Inspect each dataset with preview, shape, dtype and descriptive
summaries. Validate that match-result codes are in {H, D, A}, that
counts are non-negative, that match keys (Date + HomeTeam + AwayTeam)
are unique, and that shots on target do not exceed total shots.
Filter Premier League players and standardise team names across the
two sources. Goalkeeper-only fields for outfield players are left
missing — not imputed with an overall mean or zero.

4.2 Feature engineering and aggregation
Convert each match record into two team-perspective rows and
aggregate points, goals scored, goals conceded and shooting
activity. Compute per-match measures, shot accuracy and an aggregate
goals-to-shots ratio. Aggregate goalkeeper saves and shots faced
before deriving save rates, then reconcile totals against the
match-level source.

4.3 Analysis and visualisation
Compute Pearson and Spearman correlations between team-level
indicators and points. Produce the attack–defence two-panel figure
(Figure 1). Test paired home-versus-away differences in shots, shots
on target and corners. Summarise cards descriptively. Explore
player-level scoring against age using goals per 90 minutes with
position groups and a minutes threshold.

5. Statistical approach and visualisation

Team-level correlations. Pearson and Spearman coefficients are
computed between end-of-season points and each of: goals scored per
match, goals conceded per match, shots per match, shot accuracy,
opponent shots on target per match, and goalkeeper save rate.
Preliminary values are given in Table 1.

Table 1. Preliminary team-level correlations with points (n = 20).

    Indicator                     Pearson   Spearman
    Goals scored per match         +0.908    +0.829
    Goals conceded per match       −0.900    −0.754
    Shots per match                +0.810    +0.766
    Shot accuracy                  +0.131    +0.191
    Opponent SoT per match         −0.837    −0.635
    Save rate                      +0.417    +0.434

Predictive check. As an exploratory supplement, leave-one-out
cross-validation was run on two single-feature linear regressions:
one using goals scored per match, the other using goals conceded
per match. The defence-only model produced a lower LOO MAE
(≈ 6.35 points, R² ≈ 0.72) than the attack-only model
(≈ 8.71 points, R² ≈ 0.55). This preliminary difference is
reported for interest and will be revisited with the caveat that
n = 20 makes any advantage unstable.

Home-versus-away tests. For shots, shots on target and corners,
two-sided paired t-tests will examine whether the mean home-minus-
away within-match difference equals zero. Reported outputs include
mean differences, 95% pointwise confidence intervals, t statistics,
effect sizes (Cohen’s d for paired data) and Holm-adjusted p-values
across the three tests. Because teams recur across matches, the
independence assumption between paired differences is only an
approximation; these tests will be treated as exploratory rather
than as proof of a causal home effect.

Player-level exploration. Goals per 90 minutes are computed for
outfield players. The main eligibility threshold is 900 minutes,
with 450 and 1,350 minutes as sensitivity checks. Comparisons by
age and position are descriptive and do not isolate an independent
age effect. Red and yellow cards are summarised descriptively.

Figure 1. Same-season relationships between scoring, conceding and
calculated points (see attack_defence.png in the project folder;
please insert here in Word). Each point represents one team; the
common vertical axis makes the direction and strength of the two
relationships directly comparable, while the scatter shows that
teams with similar scoring or defensive records may still finish on
different point totals. The fitted lines describe this season only
and do not forecast future performance.


6. Data limitations and planned refinement

The goalkeeper and match sources disagree on shots-on-target totals
for all 20 teams; Chelsea also shows a two-goal difference in
goalkeeper goals conceded. Field definitions and coverage will be
investigated before any goalkeeper measure is used to support
headline conclusions.

The single-season team analysis has only 20 observations, and
goals are mechanically related to the rules used to calculate
points. Findings therefore remain descriptive and season-specific.
Multi-season replication and methods that address repeated-team
dependence are potential later extensions, not parts of this
proposal.


7. Criteria for success

Success is defined by the checklist below. Statistical significance
is not a success requirement; a clear null or weak relationship is
also a valid finding.

  [ ] Both original dataset URLs, publishers, collection dates and
      relevant field definitions are documented in the notebook.
  [ ] The notebook runs in order from a fresh kernel using only the
      two CSV files, without errors or manual edits between cells.
  [ ] Team names match across sources; match keys are unique;
      every team has 38 records; and the calculated goal and point
      totals pass reconciliation checks.
  [ ] Missing-value decisions and source discrepancies are visible
      in the notebook; unresolved goalkeeper metrics are excluded
      from substantive conclusions.
  [ ] Charts have readable titles, axis labels and units. Tables
      identify the observation level and the denominators used for
      rates.
  [ ] Paired comparisons report effect sizes, uncertainty and
      multiplicity adjustment, and state the repeated-team
      limitation.
  [ ] The report answers the main question, distinguishes
      supporting analyses, and avoids causal or predictive claims
      unsupported by the design.
